What it asks

VAAGDHARA works with women's collectives across the Bhil belt, where Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat meet. It can count how many women came to a meeting or took a loan. It wanted to know something harder. How far does each woman feel she can act, at home, in her village, in her panchayat? And is that moving?

So a facilitator sits with a woman and listens to her life story first. Then they go through a set of questions about her agency: over her land and her money, in the village and how it is governed, in her culture, and in the digital world she now has to deal with for rations, payments and papers.

The ladder

Each woman is placed on a ladder with four rungs. Participant (भागीदार). Doer (कर्ता). Motivator (प्रेरक). Leader (नेता).

When the assessment ends, the AI suggests three small tasks that could take her one rung up. The facilitator reads them and decides what to carry back to her. The next visit draws the ladder again, so everyone can see whether she moved.

Built for places with no signal

Most of these conversations happen where there is no network. The app on the facilitator's phone keeps everything on the device and sends it the next time the phone finds a connection. If the phone stays off for a week, nothing is lost.

About 120 facilitators use it. Their work lands on one dashboard, called मैं हूँ सक्षम, where VAAGDHARA can see the picture by household, village and panchayat, and where it is changing.

How it was built

React Native with Expo for the phone app. FastAPI and Postgres behind it, backed up every night. A React dashboard on top, on Vercel.
