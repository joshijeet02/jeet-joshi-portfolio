Where I come from

I grew up in Banswara, a small city in the Wagad belt of southern Rajasthan. Most people there are tribal, and most of them farm. My family does not farm, but I grew up close enough to it to learn early that an economic problem always lands on someone in particular.

I studied economics at the Gokhale Institute of Politics and Economics in Pune. In 2021 I spent a summer at the Centre for Budget and Governance Accountability in Delhi, mapping how civil society works across six states. The next year I went to Ladakh as a field investigator. I did over a hundred interviews there, and spent twelve days inside an army cantonment so I could reach the border villages.

Then I went to Sydney for a master's in financial economics. I liked the rigour of it. In my last year there I started building small AI tools, mostly because I kept seeing good analysis go nowhere, and I wanted to know if I could carry it the last mile myself.

I came back in 2025. Before moving on, I wanted to give something back to the place that raised me. So for the past year I have worked with Gagan Sethi at Alliance for Change in Ahmedabad, training the first cohort of grassroots researchers to study their own communities.

The building did not stop. In January 2026 I started Vasuman Associates, my own small practice, and built COMPASS for AARAV Fragrances & Flavors under it. For VAAGDHARA, the organisation my father founded and leads in our region, I built a grant system and, this October, the software for its leadership journey.

On writing

I write a newsletter called Chai with an Economist. About 3,000 people read it, and the Takshashila Institution and Puliyabaazi have shared my work. I write in my diary first, with a pen, then type it out a day or two later. I also translated a book by Dr. M. S. Swaminathan into Hindi.

I speak Hindi, English and Gujarati, and enough French to get by.
