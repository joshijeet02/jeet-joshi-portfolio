The problem it solves

A fragrance and flavour house has to know a lot at once. Which materials are getting dearer. Which rules are about to change. What a rival just launched. What buyers complain about after they buy. Who is filing to sell something new.

All of it gets written down somewhere, every day. Nobody on the team has time to read it all. So the knowing ends up with whoever happened to see the right thing, and walks out of the door with them.

What it does

COMPASS does the reading. Every night it goes out to its sources, brings back what is new, works out what each piece is about, and files it on a shelf the whole company shares.

Anyone at AARAV can ask it a question in their own words. The answer comes from that shelf, with the source sentence beside each claim, so it can be forwarded to a customer as it is.

What it keeps watch on

Each source was picked for a reason, read before it was added, and is collected only in ways its publisher allows. Broadly, they fall into these kinds.

The rules

The regulations and standards themselves, in full, Indian and global.

Materials and prices

Trade flows, crop and supply news, and the science of new molecules.

The industry

Trade press, launches, packaging, and what the large houses announce.

The buyer

What Indian customers write after they buy, and what creators tell them.

Early signals

Filings that show who means to sell something, long before it reaches a shelf.

The company's own work

Its own studies and reports, seen only by the people allowed to see them.

Why the team can trust it

It knows which kind of source can answer which kind of question. A buyer's review can tell you what people believe about an ingredient. It can never tell you whether that ingredient is allowed. Only the rulebook can, and COMPASS holds itself to that in its code.

When the shelf does not hold an answer, it says so, and says why. It never fills the gap with something that sounds right.

It runs on the company's own server. Nothing about its market, its questions or its documents leaves. Every night has a spending limit it cannot cross, and every morning it reports on its own health, including what went wrong.

Every company has a market like this

One it should be reading every day, and rarely has the time to. In another industry the sources would be different, but the way of reading them carries over. If you would like to talk about one for your company, write to me.
